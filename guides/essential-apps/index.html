The apps you actually need in Shanghai — and what to set up before you land

You don't need dozens of apps to get started. A handful cover most of what you need: payments, messaging, transport, navigation, translation, and everyday services. Not every popular Chinese app is equally easy for international users — here's which ones are, which work in English, which take a foreign card, and what to skip until later.

Five things to do before you fly

  1. 01 Set up payments

    Verify Alipay and WeChat, and add your international bank card where supported, before you're standing at a checkout counter.
  2. 02 Sort out connectivity

    If you need access to overseas services for work, school, or personal accounts, arrange your VPN before entering mainland China — not after.
  3. 03 Download translation tools

    Include offline dictionary data wherever possible, so menus and signs still work without signal.
  4. 04 Save addresses in Chinese

    Keep Chinese text and screenshots for your hotel, home, workplace, school, and airport destination.
  5. 05 Use a phone number you can keep accessing

    Verification codes become a real problem if your payment and travel accounts are tied to a number you later lose.

Payments & communication

WeChat and Alipay

Best for: Communication

WeChat 微信

EnglishSet up before flyingSome intl. card payments

WeChat is how much of China communicates: messages, group chats, contacts, schools, landlords, businesses, mini programs, and increasingly payments. Once you live here, a lot of daily life happens inside it.

Create and verify your account before you land — verification is easier with a stable connection and a contact who already uses WeChat, both of which are harder to arrange on arrival day.

International cards are supported for many payment functions, but some transfers, red packets, and receiving money from friends stay restricted to accounts linked to a Chinese bank card.

iOS Android
Best for: Payments + transport + mini apps

Alipay 支付宝

EnglishForeign cardsSet up before flying

Alipay is the easiest all-round newcomer app. Use it for shop and restaurant payments, transport QR codes, and a large range of city-service mini apps. A Chinese bank account is not required to start using many payment functions.

Link a supported overseas bank card and complete identity verification with your passport before you arrive — this reduces payment interruptions and can raise your transaction limits.

Alipay and WeChat overlap, but neither fully replaces the other. See whether you need both below, and our full Alipay setup guide and mobile payments guide for fees, limits, and troubleshooting.

iOS Android

Do I need both WeChat and Alipay? Yes. Use WeChat for communication, contacts, groups, and mini programs. Use Alipay for payments, transport, and services. Both can support international cards for many everyday payments, but some functions available to Chinese bank account holders will be unavailable or restricted. They overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

Getting around

Maps, transport, rides, and translation

Amap vs Baidu Maps

Choose Amap if you want the more international-user-friendly starting point, with English navigation for Chinese addresses, compound gates, walking routes, and metro journeys.

Choose Baidu Maps if you're comfortable with a more Chinese-language-heavy experience and want a second comprehensive local map.

Our pick: most newcomers only need one map app. Start with Amap, and only add Baidu Maps later if you find you want it.

Best for: Navigation

Amap 高德地图

EnglishSet up before flying

Amap is the primary mapping recommendation for newcomers. It's more useful than an overseas maps app for Chinese addresses, residential compound entrances, restaurants, walking routes, metro journeys, and local points of interest, and it has become increasingly friendly to international users with English-language navigation.

Prefer Baidu Maps instead? It's a solid alternative — just start with one rather than juggling both.

Best for: Shanghai public transport

SH MaaS 随申行

Foreign cards supported

SH MaaS is Shanghai's official all-in-one transport app — metro, buses, ferries, transport QR codes, and passes, in one place. It's the primary transport recommendation for the city, ahead of single-purpose metro apps.

The core booking and QR flows include English-language options, though expect more Chinese in secondary screens. International bank cards can also be supported.

Practical routine: use Amap to work out where you're going, then SH MaaS to actually get there. See our full Shanghai Metro guide and getting around Shanghai guide for route planning and fares.

MetroMan still works as a simple, optional metro-route planner if you want a lighter app just for line names and last-train times — but it's no longer the main transport app to reach for.

MetroMan (iOS) MetroMan (Android)
Best for: Taxis and ride-hailing

DiDi 滴滴出行

English

DiDi has English-language functionality and lets you enter a destination directly, instead of explaining an address to a driver out loud.

Do you need the standalone app? Not necessarily. Ride-hailing is also available through Alipay and WeChat's wider ecosystem. Download the standalone app if you use taxis often or prefer a dedicated interface — otherwise skip it and use what's already on your phone. Our DiDi and airport transfers guide covers booking in detail.

Shanghai tip: save your home, hotel, and workplace addresses in Chinese before your first ride. Drivers sometimes call when they arrive, and having the Chinese address ready prevents confusion.

iOS Android
Best for: Getting through daily life

A translation setup 翻译工具

Set up before flying

Don't rely on a single app. Use Pleco as a proper Chinese dictionary — words, characters, menus, signs — and pair it with Baidu Translate or another China-accessible photo/voice translator for on-the-spot conversations and signage.

Google Translate is worth keeping as an offline backup with downloaded language packs, but don't assume normal online Google functionality will be available on a standard mainland China connection.

Food & everyday life

Delivery, once you have an address

Best for: Food delivery and local services

Meituan 美团

Interface mostly Chinese

Meituan covers restaurants, takeaway, local services, and deals — much more than just food delivery.

What catches newcomers out: your delivery address must be detailed. Entering only your compound name isn't enough. Include the road, compound name, building number, floor and apartment, and a working phone number, or your order won't arrive.

iOS Android
Best for: Fast local delivery

Taobao Flash Shopping 淘宝闪购

Older China guides often recommend Ele.me — that advice is outdated. Ele.me's services have increasingly moved into Taobao Flash Shopping's branding and ecosystem, so this is the current name to look for.

Use it for meals, groceries, drinks, convenience items, and rapid local delivery. The same detailed-address rule as Meituan applies.

Good to know: you don't need both Meituan and Taobao Flash Shopping immediately. Start with one and add the other later if you want more choice.

Best for: Discovering restaurants and local places

Dianping 大众点评

Think of Dianping as restaurant discovery, local reviews, photos, and deals combined. It becomes genuinely useful once you start actively exploring your neighbourhood and the wider city.

Worth downloading on day one? No. Worth having once you live here? Yes.

Shopping

Taobao, once you're ready

Best for: Almost everything

Taobao 淘宝

Taobao eventually becomes essential for furniture, kitchenware, clothing, electronics, stationery, household products, pet supplies, and everyday essentials.

It isn't a day-one priority. Wait until you have digital payments working, a reliable Chinese address, a working phone number, and some confidence using your translation tools — Taobao is far easier to navigate once those basics are in place.

Travel beyond Shanghai

Trip.com and 12306

Best for: Easy English-language travel booking

Trip.com 携程

English

Useful for hotels, flights, and trains, Trip.com is usually the easiest starting point for foreigners booking domestic travel.

iOS Android
Best for: Booking Chinese trains directly

Railway 12306 铁路12306

12306 is China's official railway booking platform. Foreign travellers can register using passport details and book trains directly, without a booking-fee markup. Search "铁路12306" or "Railway 12306" in your app store.

Trip.com or 12306? Start with Trip.com if convenience and English support matter most. Use 12306 once you're comfortable booking directly through China's official railway system. You don't need both immediately.

New / worth knowing — Nihao China. A newer app aimed at international visitors, bundling payments, transport, and travel features in one place. Useful for short-term visitors. If you're actually living in Shanghai, WeChat and Alipay still matter far more.

Newcomer rules

Three rules that save a lot of frustration

None of these are about any single app — they're about how you set the others up.

  1. 01 Use one stable phone number

    Keep your payment, travel, delivery, and transport accounts connected to a number you can reliably access — verification codes get complicated fast otherwise.

  2. 02 Keep Chinese addresses ready

    Save your important locations as copyable Chinese text and as screenshots. Don't rely entirely on English place names.

  3. 03 Don't try to master every app

    WeChat and Alipay's mini-program ecosystem already gives you access to many services. You rarely need the standalone version of everything right away.

Quick answer

What do I actually need?

Start with WeChat, Alipay, Amap, SH MaaS, and a reliable translation setup. Add Meituan once you know your delivery address. Add Taobao once you're comfortable shopping locally. Add Trip.com or 12306 when you start travelling around China. Everything else can wait.

Frequently asked questions

Essential apps questions for foreigners

Do I need both WeChat and Alipay?

Yes. Use WeChat for communication, contacts, and groups. Use Alipay for payments, transport, and city services. They overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

Do I need to download the DiDi app separately?

Not necessarily. Ride-hailing is also available inside Alipay and WeChat. Download the standalone DiDi app only if you use taxis often or prefer a dedicated interface.

Should I use Amap or Baidu Maps?

Start with Amap. It's the more international-user-friendly option and covers Chinese addresses, metro routing, and walking directions well. Add Baidu Maps later only if you want a second option.

Do I need both Meituan and Taobao Flash Shopping?

No, not immediately. Start with one for food and convenience delivery, then add the other later if you want more choice.

When should I start using Taobao?

Wait until your payments, address, and phone number are working reliably, and you have some confidence using translation tools.

Should I book travel with Trip.com or 12306?

Start with Trip.com if English support and convenience matter most. Use 12306 once you're comfortable booking directly through China's official railway system.

Is Nihao China worth downloading?

It can be useful for short visits, since it bundles payments, transport, and travel features for international users. If you're living in Shanghai, WeChat and Alipay matter far more.

What's the biggest app mistake newcomers make?

Downloading a standalone app for every service. WeChat and Alipay already give access to many services through mini programs, so you rarely need the separate app right away.
