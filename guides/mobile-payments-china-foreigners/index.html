Payments in China

Pay in Shanghai without a Chinese bank account

Most day-to-day spending in Shanghai happens through Alipay and WeChat Pay — and both apps work with many eligible foreign bank cards. Here's how to get set up, what it costs, and what to carry as backup.

The basics

How paying by phone actually works

Payment goes one of two directions — learn both and you're set for almost everywhere in Shanghai.

You scan them

The usual way to pay in shops, cafés, and restaurants.

Open Alipay / WeChat → Scan the merchant's QR code → Enter the amount → Confirm

They scan you

Common at the metro, supermarkets, and busier counters.

Open Pay in the app → Show your payment code → Merchant scans it → Done

Money matters

What foreign-card payments actually cost

Fees and limits can change — always check the confirmation screen before you pay. As a general guide:

≤ ¥200 Often no extra platform fee

Most everyday purchases — meals, coffee, groceries, metro top-ups — fall under this line.

¥200+ A 3% fee may apply

On the portion of a single transaction above ¥200, depending on the merchant and payment method.

Card issuer May add its own fee

Check your bank's foreign transaction and currency conversion terms before relying on a card.

Worked example

A ¥350 dinner: no extra fee on the first ¥200, and a 3% fee (about ¥4.50) may apply to the remaining ¥150 — roughly ¥354.50 charged, before any fee your card issuer adds.

Good to know

Since a 2024 government update, the single-transaction limit for overseas visitors is around USD 5,000, with an annual cumulative cap near USD 50,000 — plenty for daily life. Confirm large payments such as deposits, tuition, or rent with the merchant in advance.

May not work

  • Person-to-person transfers

Treat a foreign card in Alipay or WeChat Pay as a practical spending tool, not a full replacement for a local Chinese bank account.

Paying for transport in Shanghai

When things go wrong

Common payment problems and fixes

My card won't link

Check that your card supports international online payments, and that the name on your card matches your registered name in the app. Try another card if one is available.

I can't receive the SMS code

Check roaming, mobile signal, country code, and SMS permissions. If your home number is unreliable in China, use a local SIM or a reliable travel eSIM.

The payment was declined

Try turning off your VPN, lowering the payment amount, switching from scan-to-pay to payment-code mode, or using another card.

The merchant QR code doesn't work

Ask the merchant if they can scan your payment code instead. Some personal or merchant-specific QR codes may not support foreign-card payments.

A large payment failed

Split the payment if the merchant allows it, use another card, pay with a physical card, or confirm whether the merchant accepts foreign-card mobile payments.

My app asks for passport verification

Upload your passport details clearly and make sure the information matches your account and card details.

Frequently asked questions

Mobile payment questions for foreigners

Can foreigners use Alipay in China?

Yes. Many foreigners can use Alipay by linking an eligible overseas bank card and completing identity verification. See our full Alipay guide for foreigners.

Can foreigners use WeChat Pay in China?

Yes. Many foreigners can use WeChat Pay with an eligible overseas card, but some features are limited compared with a local Chinese wallet. Our WeChat Pay guide covers the setup.

Do I need a Chinese bank account?

No, not for basic merchant payments. However, a Chinese bank account can make life easier if you live in China long term.

Do I need a Chinese phone number?

Not always. An international number can work if it receives SMS verification codes. A Chinese SIM or reliable eSIM is still helpful.

Which app should I set up first?

Set up Alipay first, then WeChat Pay. Alipay is usually easier for new arrivals, while WeChat Pay becomes more important once you start using local services.

Should I still carry cash?

Yes. Carry a small amount of RMB cash for emergencies.

Can I send money to friends with a foreign card?

Usually not. Person-to-person transfers and red packets are often limited or unavailable with foreign cards.

Can I use Apple Pay or Google Pay in Shanghai?

Do not rely on Apple Pay or Google Pay as your main payment method. Alipay and WeChat Pay are far more widely used for everyday QR-code payments.

Recent updates

China raised mobile payment limits for overseas visitors in 2024, and American Express joined Alipay's system in 2025 for eligible cardholders. PayPal also began connecting with WeChat Pay's merchant network in 2026 for U.S. users, with wider rollout expected — but Alipay and WeChat Pay should still be your main setup for now.
